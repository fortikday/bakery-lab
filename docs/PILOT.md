# A small bakery pilot

This is a proposed validation plan, not a report of customers or savings.
The first question is whether staff can keep a useful, trustworthy log without
adding too much work at closing.

## Start small

Find one bakery willing to try a voluntary two-week trial. Agree on three
products, who records each day, and who reviews the week. Use real production,
sales, donation, closing-stock, discard, and sellout quantities with permission.
Keep the bakery's records private. Do not change production automatically.

Before the trial, ask how production is decided today, what happens to leftovers,
whether these quantities are already recorded, and which decision a weekly
report could improve. Watch the existing closing workflow if the owner agrees.

## Check the workflow

- Can a staff member open the app, save a balanced entry, and correct a mistake
  without developer help? Record the time and any confusion.
- How many planned product-days were recorded? List closed days separately from
  missed entries; do not fill either with invented zero waste.
- Can the owner explain the weekly totals and find one specific question to
  discuss with the team?
- Can the owner download a backup, find it again, and recover a fictional copy
  in a separate test installation?
- Does the owner want to keep using the log? What would make it worth paying
  for, and what would stop them?

Choose acceptable time and recording-completeness targets with the bakery
before starting. Report actual results, including failed days and objections.
These are small usability observations, not statistically representative demand
or proof of a sustainable business.

## Decide the next investment

If logging is too slow, fix the workflow or investigate an existing till export.
If staff keep reliable records but cannot act on the report, improve its useful
questions. If separate devices are the barrier, scope accounts and shared
storage around that observed need.

Forecasting comes after checking that raw records are usable. Any later baking
trial should monitor both discarded food and early sellouts; lower waste alone
can hide underproduction. A before/after comparison also needs comparable
products, opening days, production volumes, and other changes recorded.

Record consented feedback and anonymized observations in project documentation.
Do not invent quotes, customer counts, contributors, or measured savings.
